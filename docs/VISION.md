## 1. The doctrine: quiet at rest, articulate in motion

**Latest builder clarification (6 October, continued work):** keep action surfaces side by side until useful reading space runs out, then wrap to the next row. Agent thinking/reasoning/replies punctuate the action flow beneath it; subsequent actions begin a new passage. Sharing a row describes presentation order, never proof of concurrent execution. Preserve each content-shaped surface's allocated width when another action arrives, rather than stretching the first across the whole row and shrinking its text later. Smooth origin-backed surface unfolding supplies the requested popping-up feeling; captured characters still arrive whole and selectable, without typewriter effects or delayed evidence.

**5 October builder emphasis:** a transcript on a different background, attractive boxes, or animations around tool-call labels do not satisfy this vision. Theme, colour and choreography are part of the product, but their purpose is to make the real work understandable side by side with the running agent. The repository must participate as an intelligible place; source, changes and outcomes must have visible relationships and distinct useful compositions. A miniature map or five named transitions alone is not proof of this standard. Review the rendered application after each substantial visual change; technical acceptance does not freeze the design or establish a market-first claim.

Two forces must both win:

- **Truth.** Drishya shows *captured evidence*. It never fakes activity, never implies causality it cannot prove, never types out text that was already recorded, never invents a location. (Engineering constraints 4 and 5; [review §3](PRODUCT_WORK_HISTORY_REVIEW_NOTES.md#01-product-review).)
- **Craft.** It must feel like a $1,000 edit, not a $10 one. Every arrival, connection, settle and selection is choreographed.

These do not conflict, and the reconciliation is the core creative idea:

> **Motion is spent on *relationships and arrivals*, never on *simulating the reading task*.**

A file does not type itself out. But it *emerges from its own place on the map*, carrying a hairline back to where it lives. A diff does not fake keystrokes — but the removed line genuinely *lifts away* and the added line *settles into its slot*, because both states were captured. The richness lives in space, material, light and connection. The content always arrives whole, selectable, immediately.

**At rest, the screen is calm and readable.** Nothing loops, pulses or drifts for attention. **In motion, everything is precise and meaningful.** A viewer should be able to pause any frame and read it — and watch any transition and understand *why* it moved.

The test for every animation: **if you removed it, would the user lose information about where something came from, what it connects to, or what changed?** If not, it is decoration — cut it or make it earn its place.

| **Map** (behind everything) | The repository as a living field of files and relationships | Deep, quiet, interactive. Light pools where work happens and slowly cools |
| **Canvas** (centre) | Action passages left→right, prose beneath, history downward | The stage. Dominant, readable, accumulating |
| **Dock** (right) | Native tools, then MCP servers by name | Instruments on a shelf. Each is a real origin point |
| **Filmstrip** (bottom) | Transport plus a minimap of the whole run | Always discoverable, never a dashboard |

**Every visual element has exactly one home, and moves from its home for a reason.** A file comes *from the map*. A tool call comes *from the dock*. A thought comes *from the agent*. That is the entire spatial grammar, and it is what makes the screen legible without labels.

## 3. The signature moves

Five moves carry the product. Each is specified in detail in the [continuous canvas](reference/04-continuous-canvas.md) and [experience spec](PRODUCT_WORK_HISTORY_REVIEW_NOTES.md#02-experience-spec); this is their creative intent.

### 3.1 Lift — content comes out of the map

The file's node on the map brightens and **draws a hairline filament** toward the canvas. Along it, a surface **unfolds from the node's side** — the frame grows, the content fades in *at final size* as it settles. A 1 px tether stays, curving back to the node. Hover the surface and the tether and node brighten together; hover the node and every surface from that file, across the whole history, glows in answer.

*This is the moat made visible.* A tool with no repository model has nowhere for content to come *from*. One second of motion shows the difference without a word.

### 3.2 Emit — a tool call leaves its instrument

The tool's mark in the right dock resolves into focus. The named MCP server and its observed tools remain readable at rest; the active instrument emits a thin ribbon **inward** to its allocated place in the passage. The earlier drawer metaphor describes focus and arrival, not permission to hide the tool list (6 October clarification). The surface starts holding captured arguments; when the result returns, **the same surface grows** to its measured reading size. No second card flies in from nowhere. The ribbon fades to a faint filament linked to the actual visible tool, with a qualified server fallback when that tool is outside the dock viewport.

### 3.3 Settle — work accumulates like pages on a desk

When the next operation arrives, the previous one doesn't vanish. It **eases back** through restrained contrast and overlap only at its *margin*, with its title and a meaningful excerpt still readable. Do not shrink text to fit more. After a passage, the whole row settles into its retained layout. Select any settled surface and it rises for reading; live follow holds while history keeps accumulating. Optional sequence playback holds too.

### 3.4 Surface — prose rises beneath the work

When the agent speaks, the finished passage settles and the agent's actual words appear beneath it, **unboxed** — plain readable text in the UI sans, no bubble, no card, with a single agent-coloured rule at the left edge and the agent's name above. The paragraph arrives complete and **resolves into focus**, a subtle blur-to-sharp like a camera pulling focus, so it is readable instantly and never typewritten. Then the next passage opens below. This vertical rhythm — *action, action, action · words · action, action* — is the product's heartbeat.

### 3.5 Resolve — the outcome lands once

A test run is a fine row of tick marks, one per test, filled at their true counts. A failure is a **single sharp red notch** — and the assertion opens directly from that notch: expected, actual, the line. A passing rerun resolves the notch back to a quiet tick. An edit's removed line **lifts and dissolves** while the added line **settles into its slot**, alignment preserved. Every outcome changes once, precisely, and then holds still.

## 4. Each action has its own shape

The rule from your vision — *not boxes* — means **no generic card template**. Each kind of work has a distinct composition, so a glance tells you what happened.

| Action | Its shape | What makes it feel alive |
|---|---|---|
| **Read** | A slice cut from the file: the read lines bright, with **abstract ghost shapes above and below** fading out — you *see* it is a window into a larger file. The ghosts are blurred bars, **never real text**: bytes outside the captured range were not captured, so showing them would invent content | Lifts from its map node; the ghosts convey "excerpt" without a label |
| **Search** | The query on a fine luminous line; results **hang from it like beads**, each an excerpt with its path | Each bead is linked to its map location by a hairline; hover a bead, its file lights up |
| **Edit** | A diff with line alignment preserved: removed lines struck and dimmed, added lines warm | The removed line **lifts away**, the new line **settles into place**, using the real captured before and after |
| **Shell** | The command on a baseline like a prompt; output falls beneath it in a monospace field | Output arrives at real captured chunk boundaries live; exit status resolves as one mark |
| **Test** | A row of fine ticks: passes quiet, failures as red notches | The failing assertion opens from its notch |
| **MCP** | Server and tool as a quiet attribution, arguments, then the result in its natural shape | Emerges from its named server in the dock and stays tethered to it |
| **Prose** | Editorial text directly on the darkened world | Focus-pull reveal; agent glyph at the margin |
| **Subagent** | A smaller presence **splits from its parent** in the crown | A lane opens in history; the parent keeps its place |
| **Approval** | The request with where to respond | Only *that agent's* lane holds; the rest of the world keeps moving |
| **Gap / unknown** | What is known, what is missing, stated plainly | A neutral, dignified state — missing evidence is shown, never papered over |

## 5. Theme: a clean developer tool

*Revised 21 Sep after review of the first prototype: the functionality and what it shows were right; the look was too busy. Glow pools, blur, grain, a serif voice and five accents read as messy. The standard is now the tools developers already trust — Linear, Vercel, GitHub.*

**Precise, content-shaped, recognizable.** The integrated base retains its neutral palette while the builder's preferred older prototype surfaces guide the next pass. Tinted studies explore petrol ink/jade, copper changes and lilac external tools, but are not the chosen visual identity. Bounded surfaces with a small radius, a distinct material backing, restrained border/shadow and useful header are welcome when they aid reading. Agent prose stays on the world. Keep the app frame clear; no decorative glow pools, frosted blur, grain or vignettes.

**"No boxes" means no *labelled* boxes.** What the builder rejected is the `model thinking:` / `tool call:` / `output:` stack. A clean, content-shaped surface for a file read or a diff is the right developer idiom. The agent's prose stays unboxed: plain text with a single agent-coloured rule at its left edge.

**Colour keeps a role.** Structure uses coordinated ink tints; brighter colours identify navigation/material/agent roles. Colour is reserved for:

| Meaning | Colour |
|---|---|
| Agent identity | One distinct stable hue per agent; prototype Claude copper, Explore lilac, Codex jade. Existing integrated identity assignments remain stable |
| Pass / added | `#86D8AD`, with a restrained green line wash |
| Fail / removed | `#F09B99`, with a restrained red line wash |
| Edited file / change material | Copper `#EDB783` |
| Interactive focus / source reading rail | Jade `#7DE0C5` |
| MCP / external material | Lilac `#C0A9ED` |

**Study tokens, not the selected production palette:** dark base `#0B1B1E`, chrome `#0E2326`, source `#10282B`, surfaces `#173337` / `#204146`, hairline `#294345`, text `#E6F2EC` / `#A1BBB6` / `#829F99`. Relay uses blue ink `#101F31` / `#14283D`; Atlas uses sage paper `#EEEEDE` / `#E4E9D8` and darker role colours for light-background contrast. Prototype primary/secondary/caption tokens are checked at ≥4.5:1 against the base, source and chrome. This token check is not a whole-app accessibility certificate. Integrated neutral tokens remain in `styles.css`. Existing code syntax remains distinct from UI role colours. Colour never carries meaning alone; shape and label always back it up. The original `docs/prototype/index.html` remains untouched and is the stronger builder-preferred presentation reference.

**Type** — one sans (Inter / Geist, system fallback) for UI and prose, one mono (Geist Mono / JetBrains Mono / Cascadia) for code and paths. No serif, no italics, no letter-spaced uppercase labels. Sentence case everywhere.

**The map is quiet.** The run's files form a spine in the left gutter; the rest of the repository fades out from behind the reading column so no line ever crosses text. Heat is a small, tight glow and a ring for edited files — information, never atmosphere.

**Motion is unchanged** — Lift, Emit, Settle, Surface, Resolve — but the travelling shell now looks like the surface itself arriving, and nothing blooms. Depth comes from opacity and a single shadow, not from scale.

## 6. Micro-interactions — "every single little thing"

**6 October integrated refinement:** The React app now uses warm graphite (`#131516` world, `#1c2021` material), jade source/navigation (`#92d6c3`), copper edits and lilac MCP (`#c4b4e6`), with darker corresponding role colours on light paper. This supersedes the neutral-only production note above; the old petrol/blue studies remain references. Distinction sits on the material's reading edge and command backing, not separate coloured panels. Agent identity stays stable. Relationship/resolve cues read the same theme tokens; prose remains unboxed with its agent rule. Short hover transitions change chrome, never captured characters or geometry. This is an implemented candidate, not a human preference certificate.

The $1,000 feel lives in the details people only notice unconsciously. Every one of these must be precise, brief and *reversible*.

- **Hover a line of code** → its file's map node brightens; the tether lights.
- **Hover/focus a map node** → visible surfaces from that file gain a restrained outline; offscreen matches remain navigable.
- **Hover a diff line** → a peek of the other state (before/after).
- **Hover a settled surface** → it lifts a hair and its full title comes into focus.
- **Select** → the surface gains a clear hairline and reading space; live follow detaches without stopping history. Close it to restore the same anchor.
- **Copy** → a brief confirmation beside the selected range, without moving its text.
- **Dock item fires** → a short ignition, a ribbon, then quiet.
- **Agent tool in flight** → a static pending mark at its crown presence; completion resolves once. No idle orbit or pulse.
- **Scrub** → the whole world rewinds together: surfaces un-settle, the map's heat recedes to that moment.
- **Focus with keyboard** → an elegant focus ring that matches the material, never the browser default.

**Interruptibility is part of the craft.** Deliberate intent — selecting, scrolling, reading, focusing a control — stops viewport following and optional replay advancement instantly. Capture and available live history continue. Merely moving the pointer does not detach follow. Nobody ever waits for an animation to finish.

## 7. The anti-list

These make a product look cheap or dishonest. They are banned.

- Equal-sized rounded cards in a grid. Borders around every event. Window title bars. Chat bubbles.
- `model thinking:` / `tool call:` / `output:` labels as the primary presentation.
- Typewriter effects on text that was already captured. Counting-up numbers. Fake scanning lines.
- Glow on everything, neon everywhere, perpetual particles, animated wallpaper, "holographic" machinery.
- A camera that chases the agent while the user is trying to read.
- Any motion implying a cause, a runtime path or a location the evidence does not support.
- Shrinking source text to fit more on screen.

## 8. The standard

Before any visual work ships, it passes three tests:

1. **The freeze test** — pause on any frame. Is every piece of text readable and every element's origin clear?
2. **The truth test** — does any motion imply something the captured evidence does not support? If so, it goes.
3. **The ten-minute test** — after ten minutes of real reading, interrupting and scrubbing, does it still feel calm, or is it tiring? The opening animation is not the product; the tenth minute is.

And one aspiration that sits above all three: **someone watching over your shoulder should stop and ask what that is.**

### The premium bar — builder direction, 29 October 2026

Hold this for every screen, every pass and every agent. It is as binding as the three tests above.

1. **Clean in every term, down to the smallest thing.** Alignment, spacing, truncation, icon weight, focus and hover states, scrollbars, counts, labels, and the empty, loading and failure states of every surface must look clean and premium. People keep using a product that feels this cared-for without knowing why; one rough edge (a name cut off beside empty space, a control larger than its content, a label that does not say what it is, a list you must page through one page at a time) makes them doubt everything else. "Small" is never a reason to leave it.
2. **Information arrives with ease.** Summary before detail; reach what you need without clicking item by item; the text on screen says what it is in the product's own words. Features that exist but present badly are not finished (AUDIT-1 finds them).
3. **The target is a $10,000, end-to-end product for millions of people** who today only read an agent's text and do not know what it is doing. The scope keeps widening to the one place a developer uses for any task (Workspace, Files, Git graph, Changes, branches, side-by-side comparison, pull requests) with the agent's work shown visually as it happens (VIZ-1). Build so that nobody can match us the next day in agent visualization and developer ease at one place.
4. **Order of work (the "Order after SCREEN-1" note in the [Product Work History](PRODUCT_WORK_HISTORY.md)):** SCREEN-1, then AUDIT-1 (use every built function, list what is wrong), then the UI passes, then the motion passes (motion is tuned on a finished UI), then the fresh-eyes agent review (COMP-1), then VIZ-1. First the base.
5. **Concept before components (30 October).** A surface is designed from what it is for, not from the controls it needs: what the developer must understand there, what identifies each item, what goes where and how much room it gets, which edges it needs, how a window opens and what the user returns to. A view of a file's contents and its history is a different composition from a list of runs. Text in a box with the usual buttons is not a design. The pass that does this over every surface is UI-8, with the brief in [review/ui-pass-brief.md](PRODUCT_WORK_HISTORY_REVIEW_NOTES.md#ui-pass-brief).

Branches share this material, weighted by loaded segment population and capped at16px. ## 9. Clarity — the look of the finished product (Yash, 6 November 2026)

Yash, on the Home concepts: "clean and modern and takes accurate space, making it minimal but professional and premium. I want that look for the whole product." Clarity is that look, built as its own UI version beside Modern ([clarity-1-version.md](PRODUCT_WORK_HISTORY_REVIEW_NOTES.md#clarity-1-version)). It refines §5 and does not replace the doctrine.

- **One face for the interface.** Inter, bundled locally, at 13 px for interface body, 12 px for meta and labels, and 15 or 20 px for the few titles. JetBrains Mono is only for code, paths, branches and commands. Tabular figures wherever numbers sit in columns. Sentence case everywhere: no tracked capitals and no kicker labels.
- **Space is measured, not filled.**
  - **Content:** sits in a measured column (Home 1160 px). Wide screens get a second pane, not stretched rows.
  - **Spacing:** comes from the 4-based scale.
  - **Rows:** have honest heights (44 px for a list row, 30 px for a table header).
  - **Alignment:** everything aligns to a few vertical lines.
- **Hairlines, not boxes.**
  - **Separation:** a list is rows divided by 1 px lines, not cards in a panel in a page. One container level at most.
  - **Floating surfaces:** a card or border only for something that floats (Running now, menus, dialogs) or that the user can open.
- **Colour only for meaning.**
  - **Ground:** black or graphite, with three ink levels (`--t1` to `--t3`).
  - **Identity:** each agent has one colour (Claude Code warm, Codex cool).
  - **Outcomes:** green for added or passed, red for removed or failed, amber for stopped or edited.
  - **Accent:** for the selected item and the single primary action only.
- **Every fact earns its place.** A number is shown because a developer acts on it. Paths are shown only where they tell two things apart. A count that is not known says so plainly ("Not opened yet") and never guesses.
- **The same control everywhere.** One button system, one menu, one select, one switch, one field and one tooltip. Each has every state, and each open has its close (FINISH-0).
